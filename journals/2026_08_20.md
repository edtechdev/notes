	- >In online learning environments, **tasks with higher cognitive complexity are associated with significantly lower student grades** [1, 2].
	- >Researchers measured cognitive complexity using Bloom's Taxonomy, which categorizes cognitive processes from basic knowledge recall up to higher-order thinking skills (HOTS) like synthesis and evaluation [3, 4]. The study revealed that **students scored significantly lower on highly complex tasks requiring synthesis and evaluation compared to moderately complex tasks that only required application** [5].
	- >The negative impact of cognitive complexity on grades in online courses is attributed to several factors:
	  * **Inherent Difficulty:** Higher-order tasks demand critical analysis, synthesis, and evaluative judgment, which are naturally more challenging than rote memorization or basic application [2, 3].
	  * **Environmental Challenges:** The online setting often amplifies the difficulty of complex tasks due to limited real-time instructional support, difficulties in accessing resources, and fewer opportunities for collaborative problem-solving with peers [6].
	  * **Delayed Feedback:** The asynchronous format common in many online courses reduces the immediacy of feedback, which is otherwise critical for mastering high-level cognitive skills [6].
	- >Despite these challenges, higher-order thinking tasks remain essential for developing students' critical and creative thinking [6]. To counteract the negative impact on grades, **the sources emphasize that instructors must carefully design cognitively complex tasks with strong scaffolding and support mechanisms** to ensure that all students can engage with the material effectively [1, 6, 7].
